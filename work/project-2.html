background

What is AiWalker?

AiWalker is a lower-limb rehabilitation exoskeleton by Beijing Ai-Robotics (北京大艾机器人) — the device that carried a torch at the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics — built for SCI, stroke, and cerebral-palsy patients relearning to walk.

This project reframes it as a full-cycle healthcare service: not a device you buy, but one continuous experience that carries a patient from supervised therapy in the hospital, through the fragile handoff, to independent recovery at home. As China pushes rehabilitation into the home, the hard problem stops being the hardware and becomes the continuity of care around it.

identifying the problem

Where the full cycle breaks

01

The Handoff Gap

Operating knowledge lives in the clinic. It doesn't travel home with the device — a manual can't replace a therapist standing beside you.

02

Untrained Operators

Overnight, the daily operator changes from a trained therapist to a family caregiver who has never set up a medical device in their life.

03

Care Goes Dark

Once home, no one sees progress or setbacks until the next clinic visit — if there even is one. Recovery happens unobserved.

the user

Who is the user?

One device, two very different people, at two very different moments. A recovering patient who was never meant to operate medical hardware — and a family caregiver who inherits that job the day they leave the hospital, with a therapist watching from a distance, if at all.

To ground the service I ran a 103-user multi-region survey, mapping where people actually dropped off. The pain wasn't the walking — it was everything around it once the professionals were gone.

competitive analysis

How does the market solve it?

I benchmarked 20+ competing rehabilitation devices and companies. Almost every one competes on the same axis: more motors, finer sensors, smarter gait algorithms. The race is a hardware race.

Almost no one designs the service around the device once it leaves the clinic — onboarding, home-mode simplicity, remote check-ins, a way back to a professional when something goes wrong. That absence is the opportunity: the gap isn't another hardware spec, it's the service wrapper nobody had built.
